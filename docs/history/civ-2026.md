# CIV 2026 history

We built this project for a three-hour robotics data hackathon, then used the event feedback to make it reusable. GitHub issues and pull requests hold the decisions, implementation work, and verification evidence. The event-day account below comes from the organizer's retrospective.

The [planning archive](planning/README.md) is the index for past maps, issue notes, prototypes, and research. For current usage, start with the [participant QuickStart](../quickstart.md), [organizer guide](../organizers/README.md), or [contributor guide](../../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## From brainstorming to two specifications, July 2026

We began by exploring what participants should investigate, which recordings and open tools could support it, and how to evaluate the result. We worked through two Wayfinder maps before building the complete workflow.

The first map defined the challenge and its technical and organizer requirements: datasets, EventSpecs, evidence, scoring, distribution, and permissions. It also produced the participant and organizer handoff site. [PR #1](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/pull/1) records those decisions. The second map defined how people would experience the day: pacing, participant guidance, coaching, recovery, submissions, and the closing session. [PR #2](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/pull/2) records that design and its participant-site changes.

The first implementation specification, [the integrated Phase 1 workflow](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/3), brought them together so the instructions, CLI, submission bundle, and organizer evaluator would agree.

## Building and checking the workflow, August to early September 2026

Implementation proceeded through five phases. Each phase exercised the workflow before the next added more material or operational requirements.

| Phase | What we implemented | GitHub record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | An offline path through inspection, detection, clips, scoring, reporting, intake, and batch evaluation on Kashiwa and Reims inputs. | [Integrated workflow](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/3) |
| 2 | Stable participant contracts, complete EventSpec expressions and resources, readiness checks, examples, layouts, and a first-run guide. | [Interface freeze and golden path](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/14) |
| 3 | Reduced participant-safe recordings, source-bound human-reviewed truth, and two-dataset evaluation and throughput checks. | [Data and evaluation](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/23) |
| 4 | The offline kit, participant site and desk materials, submission packaging, and private organizer operations and jury guidance. | [Attendee experience packaging](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/37) |
| 5 | Release-artifact checks, workstation and organizer rehearsals, recovery materials, and legal and camera-context evidence. | [Release rehearsal and go/no-go](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/47) |

The final pre-event changes included [extension-aware v2 submissions](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/54). These carried bounded resources, provenance, agent traces, and regression-test evidence while retaining accepted v1 compatibility. The linked issues and their acceptance records preserve the original environments, dates, artifact identities, and remaining limits.

## The live hackathon, 3 September 2026

We ran the hackathon in Miyazaki on 3 September 2026. Participants investigated recordings, developed detectors, and prepared evidence and reports within the three-hour session.

Time constraints changed the closing session. The first three participants whose submissions had been scored presented their findings and detectors. The discussion also included feedback on the agentic work they had done.

## Breakout feedback and the decision to support reuse

After the hackathon, a breakout session considered improvements to CIV more broadly, including the hackathon. One example was a three-month data challenge with progressive dataset releases and participant scoring at each release. The [format and acknowledgements decision](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/66) records that proposal and credits the contributors.

We chose to incorporate the feedback into simpler, documented material that another organizer could adapt when the duration or delivery format changed. The longer challenge remained a proposed adaptation, with its schedule, data releases, and evaluation policy for future organizers to define.

## Post-event rework, October 2026

The [post-session refinement map](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/56) turned the feedback into decisions and bounded implementation work. It addressed simpler participant and organizer guidance, connected-first setup with offline recovery, navigation, attribution, and code cleanup. The [documentation decision](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/60) established short audience entry points and canonical Markdown for kit copies and website summaries. The [practice-label decision](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/64) and [organizer acquisition workflow](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/65) clarified reviewed practice references and private evaluation truth.

The later [implementation map](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/109) organized delivery through four overlapping epics: [recordings and truth workflows](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/110), [portable code and participant materials](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/111), [installation and recovery](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/112), and [materials, history, and release evidence](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/113). These tracks covered different dependencies and ran alongside each other. References to "Phase 6" or "next-wave" in working notes describe this continuation.

By mid-October, the maps recorded completion of those epics and their accepted checks. Installation and recovery evidence covered native Windows AMD64 and local WSL2 Linux AMD64. Bare-host Linux and externally enforced network denial remained unverified.

## Rehearsal and documentation closeout, late October 2026

Further work added the [organizer cohort-scoring workflow](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/pull/169), binding session setup, intake, and trusted replay to one saved evaluation plan. A private end-to-end rehearsal then exposed gaps in the materials and workflow. Corrections covered [detection diagnostics](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/pull/170), [complete reproduction recipes](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/pull/171), [inert bonus evidence](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/pull/172), and [organizer-selected kit materials](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/pull/173).

With the agreed rework complete, the [current documentation pass](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/pull/174) separates the past journey from the guides used to run or adapt another session. It keeps the planning artifacts together in history, retains the reasons and evidence on GitHub, and shortens the active instructions.

## Record scope and release boundaries

Historical acceptance and human approvals apply to their exact inputs, audience, and use. They do not grant another session data rights, wider platform support, or release approval. Dataset-specific source reports and records with unresolved private-path or source-derived disclosure findings remain outside this overview.

[Payload and history clearance](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/119) and [release acceptance](https://github.com/Forerunner2012/civ2026-data-hackathon/issues/68) retain their recorded scope. Publication, kit or private-data distribution, deployment, and repository visibility remain separate actions. Archiving a file neither removes earlier Git objects nor authorizes disclosure of its inputs.
