# Is the agenda three hours or three and a half?

> Historical planning. Follow the [archive index](../../README.md) for current guides and record boundaries.

Type: grilling
Status: resolved

## Question

`HACKATHON-HANDOFF.md` §8: 3-hour fixed agenda, or 3.5 hours with a longer pitch and quality
review?

§4.2 currently allocates the three hours as 30/30/45/30/30/15, and §4.2 notes that extra time
should go to investigation and pitch review — never to installation.

What has to be settled:

- Is the slot length actually ours to choose, or is it fixed by the CIV 2026 programme? (This
  may be a fact rather than a decision — check the programme before grilling.)
- If 3.5 hours: does the extra 30 minutes go to the Challenge stage (failure investigation) or
  to Transfer (pitch and review)?
- How many teams present, and how long does turnover between pitches actually take? Six teams
  at three minutes plus turnover does not fit in the current 15-minute Transfer block.
- Does the BYOD decision in ticket 04 demand a buffer at the front for setup failures — and if
  so, is that buffer inside the slot or a pre-event session?

## Why this is on the frontier

Nothing blocks it, and it constrains the run-of-show, the coach guide and the checkpoint
timings currently sitting in the map's fog.

## Answer

**Not a decision — settled by the published programme.** Verified at
`https://civ-summerschool.org/` on 30 July 2026:

- **CIV 2026: 31 August – 4 September 2026, Miyazaki City, Japan.**
- **Hackathon: Thursday 3 September, 09:30 – 12:30.** Three hours, fixed. The 3.5-hour option
  never existed.
- **12:30 – 14:30 lunch break.**
- **14:30 – 15:00 "Hackathon results".**

Two consequences that change the design rather than merely confirming it:

1. **There is a separate 30-minute results slot after lunch.** §4.2 currently spends the last 15
   minutes of the three hours on Transfer. It does not have to: judging and pitches can move to
   14:30–15:00, which buys back 15 minutes of investigation *and* gives teams the two-hour lunch
   to prepare a pitch instead of writing it under the clock. The run-of-show should be rebuilt
   around 09:30–12:30 producing a submission, and 14:30–15:00 presenting it.
2. **The three hours are for producing a submission, not presenting one.** The ladder in §4.2
   should end at a complete, reproducible submission at 12:30; the pitch belongs in the
   afternoon slot.

Also settled by the same source: the organizing committee includes both Manabu Tsukada
(University of Tokyo, and the URT-Kashiwa corresponding author — see ticket 05) and Matthieu
Carré (Heex Technologies).

Still open: whether a pre-event setup session exists for the BYOD environment risk accepted in
ticket 04. The programme shows no such slot, so it would be remote and voluntary beforehand.
