# What language is the room in?

Type: grilling
Status: resolved

## Question

**Nobody has asked this.** It is not inherited fog from map one — map one never raised it.
[Ticket 20](../../civ2026-challenge-spec/issues/20-cohort-skill-floor-licensing.md) settled cohort,
skill floor and licensing, and language never came up.

The facts that make it a real question:

- CIV 2026 runs **31 August – 4 September 2026 in Miyazaki, Japan**
  ([ticket 08](../../civ2026-challenge-spec/issues/08-agenda-duration.md)).
- **T-Lab participates in the hackathon** — a direct collaboration with the publishing lab
  ([ticket 19](../../civ2026-challenge-spec/issues/19-personal-data-clearance.md)).
- The cohort is **30 practitioners** at an international conference
  ([ticket 20](../../civ2026-challenge-spec/issues/20-cohort-skill-floor-licensing.md)); the mix of
  local and international attendees is not recorded anywhere.
- The hero dataset is Japanese (Kashiwa); the transfer dataset is French (Reims). The domain
  vocabulary is English by default and the toolchain is entirely English.

This is small, cheap to settle now, and expensive to discover in Phase 4 when every artifact has
already been written once.

## What it decides, concretely

- **Delivery language** for the brief, the checkpoint calls and the results block. A pace car that
  narrates in one language while a third of the room works in another is not a pace car.
- **Materials.** Which of [14](14-participant-materials.md)'s artifacts are bilingual. The cheap
  answer is *the brief and the report template, nothing else*; the expensive answer is all of them;
  the wrong answer is deciding artifact by artifact after they are written.
- **Coach coverage** ([02](02-help-protocol.md)). If any participant is more comfortable in
  Japanese, at least one coach must be — and that is a **staffing** constraint with a lead time, not
  a translation task.
- **`report.md`.** What language may a participant write in? It carries **45 of 100 points**. If
  submissions may be in Japanese, [10](10-jury-composition.md)'s jury must include someone who reads
  Japanese, and split reading must route accordingly. If English is required, say so in the
  pre-event pack, because discovering it at 12:00 costs someone the points the event is most trying
  to reward.
- **The vocabulary boundary crosses languages too.** The banned terms from
  [ticket 07](../../civ2026-challenge-spec/issues/07-clean-room-boundary.md) have Japanese
  equivalents; the clean-room line is about meaning, not spelling.

## The fact to establish first

**Who is actually coming.** The local/international split of the 30 is not recorded in any ticket.
If it is knowable — from the conference's registration, from the CIV organizers, from T-Lab — get it
before deciding, because it turns this from a judgement call into arithmetic. If it is not knowable
before the day, decide the **robust** answer rather than the optimal one: the design that works
either way.

## Why this is on the frontier

Nothing blocks it, it is quick, and it blocks [14](14-participant-materials.md). It also carries a
staffing lead time through [02](02-help-protocol.md), which is the kind of constraint that is free
now and impossible in August.

## Answer

**Professional English throughout. C1 as the lower bar, and it is an established precedent — CIV
2025 ran the same way.**

Answered by the organizer, and the precedent is what makes it safe: this is not an assumption about
the cohort, it is an observation of the previous edition of the same event with the same audience.

### What it settles, in one pass

- **Delivery.** The opening briefing ([05](05-warm-up-and-environment-gate.md)) is in English. No
  bilingual calling, no interpretation.
- **Materials.** [The attendee page](17-attendee-page.md) and everything in
  [14](14-participant-materials.md) are **monolingual English**. No translation workstream, which
  removes a Phase 4 cost nobody had budgeted.
- **Coach coverage.** No language constraint on staffing. [02](02-help-protocol.md)'s floor of 3–4
  coaches stands unchanged — this was the item with a real lead time, and it is now free.
- **`report.md` is written in English**, which matters because it carries **45 of 100 points**.
  [10](10-jury-composition.md) therefore needs no non-English reading capacity.
- **The clean-room vocabulary boundary** from
  [ticket 07](../../civ2026-challenge-spec/issues/07-clean-room-boundary.md) only has to hold in one
  language, which makes it enforceable by the same term list everywhere.

### The one thing to still do

**Say it in the pre-event pack** ([07](07-pre-event-readiness-and-reveal.md)). C1 professional
English is unremarkable for this audience, but a participant discovering at 12:00 that the report
must be in English would lose the points the event most wants to reward. It costs one line.

### Not a concern, and worth recording as such

A **C1 lower bar** means the report's 45 points are not a writing test in disguise. Rubric anchors
([10](10-jury-composition.md)) should reward *what is claimed and whether it is supported*, never
fluency — which becomes materially more important given that ticket's jury composition.
